# Military Personnel Coverage Gaps — 2001

This audit reviews the 11 game-country rows that still lack a comparable, row-level source for active military personnel in the 2001 scenario. A published cap, an unverified payroll, reserve enrollment, a factional combatant range, or a partial corps count is not treated as an active-force total. The existing scenario values remain unchanged unless a source supports the same population and scope.

| ID | Source evidence reviewed | Why it does not supply a comparable active-personnel total |
|---|---|---|
| AFG | The UK House of Commons research paper *11 September 2001: the response* reports Taliban forces at 40,000–45,000, including an estimated 8,000–12,000 foreign fighters, and Northern Alliance forces at roughly 22,000–25,000. It cites contemporary Jane's reporting. [Report, pp. 47 and 54](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/RP01-72/RP01-72.pdf). | These are uncertain combatant ranges for opposing factions, not a single national active-force count. One range includes foreign fighters; combining both factions would also turn the field into a country-wide conflict roster rather than a national military series. No midpoint is imported. |
| ATA | The [Antarctic Treaty, Article I](https://www.ats.aq/e/antarctictreaty.html) reserves Antarctica for peaceful purposes and prohibits military bases, manoeuvres, and weapons testing, while permitting military personnel and equipment for scientific research or other peaceful purposes. | Antarctica is not a sovereign state with a national armed force. The treaty does not establish that no military personnel were present for peaceful support, so zero would overstate what the source proves. |
| KOS | UNMIK Regulation 1999/8 established the Kosovo Protection Corps as a civilian emergency service, with a ceiling of 3,000 active and 2,000 reserve members. [Regulation 1999/8](https://unmik.unmissions.org/sites/default/files/regulations/02english/E1999regs/RE1999_08.htm). A January 2002 Secretary-General report describes an approximate strength of 4,300 and a planned reduction to 3,000 by the end of that year. [S/2002/62, para. 15](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/456521/files/S_2002_62-EN.pdf). A later report says the approved peacetime establishment list had been reviewed in December 2001, but does not give a 2001 headcount. [S/2002/611, para. 16](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&s=S%2F2002%2F611&t=pdf). | The published figures describe an emergency-service organization, not a Kosovo armed force. The 4,300 value is an approximate 2002 strength, and the 3,000 figure is an authorized ceiling. The establishment-list review date is not an observation date. These candidates are not entered as military personnel. |
| MCO | The Monaco government's 2002 statistical yearbook reports 112 Carabiniers du Prince (3 officers, 15 NCOs, 94 enlisted personnel). [Monaco en chiffres 2002, section 3-2](https://www.monacostatistics.mc/content/download/12729/159611/file/Monaco%20en%20chiffres%202002.pdf). | This is an official 2002 count for one corps only. The same yearbook separately lists the Sapeurs-Pompiers under the Public Force but gives their 2001 activity totals, not personnel strength. The 112 therefore is neither an exact 2001 count nor a complete count of Monaco's military-status corps. |
| MNE | The OSCE Yearbook 2001 records 108,700 active personnel for Yugoslavia (Serbia and Montenegro) as one federal entry. [OSCE Yearbook 2001, Annex, entry 55](https://www.ifsh.de/file-CORE/documents/yearbook/english/01/Annex_2001.pdf). | The source does not allocate the federal force between Serbia and Montenegro. Splitting it by population, location, or an assumed share would fabricate a country-level count. |
| PSE | The 2001 *Child Soldiers Global Report* says the Oslo framework allowed up to 30,000 Palestinian police across the West Bank and Gaza; the Palestinian Authority did not declare actual force numbers, and the report notes widespread “ghosting.” [Country report](https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/cscoal/2001/en/64736). | The 30,000 is an authorized ceiling, not an observed active headcount. Payroll and later security-service estimates use uncertain or broader scopes and cannot replace it. |
| SLB | The CIA World Factbook 2001 says Solomon Islands had no regular military forces but lists the National Reconnaissance and Surveillance Force and Royal Solomon Islands Police. [Factbook 2001 entry](https://www.thefactbook.org/solomon-islands/2001). The later Truth and Reconciliation Commission summary describes the reconnaissance force as armed and tasked with border protection. [TRC report summary](https://solomons.gov.sb/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/TRC-FINAL-Report-Summary-Condensed-Version.pdf). | “No regular military” does not establish zero for the paramilitary/security bodies included by a broader active-force definition. Their 2001 headcount is not available in the reviewed sources. |
| SMR | The 2001 *Child Soldiers Global Report* identifies two small armed-forces units, the Gendarmerie and Guardie di Rocca, but publishes no strength. [Country report](https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/cscoal/2001/en/64061). The CIA Factbook lists a Voluntary Military Force and Police Force. [Factbook 2001 entry](https://memresearch.org/public/cia/factbook_2001/geos/sm.html). | The OSCE Yearbook 2001 table labels active armed forces “none” ([entry 42](https://www.ifsh.de/file-CORE/documents/yearbook/english/01/Annex_2001.pdf)), while the contemporaneous country report identifies two military units. The difference reflects a scope/definition issue; the zero cannot be used as a total for the units described in the 2001 report. |
| SOM | In July 2001, the Transitional National Government's defence minister said about 10,000 former soldiers and recruits were registered, while explicitly stating that he could not give an accurate number. [IRIN report, 24 July 2001](https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/report/23557/somalia-committees-oversee-formation-national-army). A U.S. State Department report says former soldiers were registering and enrolling in training camps to form an army. [Country report on 2001](https://2001-2009.state.gov/g/drl/rls/hrrpt/2001/af/8403.htm). | Registration included former soldiers and recruits in training, and the minister disclaimed an accurate count. This is not an observed active-force total. |
| SSD | South Sudan seceded from Sudan in 2011, so the modern country did not exist as a sovereign military roster in 2001. [United Nations country history](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/member-states/south-sudan). The 2001 U.S. State Department country report describes the SPLA as a rebel force operating in southern Sudan. [Sudan report, 2001](https://www.refworld.org/reference/annualreport/usdos/2002/21752). | A later South Sudan country record cannot be projected backward as an independent 2001 army. SPLA and allied-force estimates are conflict-group estimates, not a comparable national active-force count. |
| PRI | Puerto Rico had no separate sovereign armed forces. Its Army National Guard is part of the U.S. National Guard reserve component; the GAO's 2001 strength review lists Puerto Rico within the Army National Guard. [GAO-02-540R](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a91173.html). | Reserve-component strength is not active-duty strength, and federal U.S. personnel stationed in Puerto Rico are not a Puerto Rico national military. Neither can be assigned as a separate active-force count for this game-country row. |

## Disposition

No new military-personnel values are imported from these candidates. The reviewed sources confirm why the current scenario values remain unsupported for this field; they do not provide a complete observation with matching 2001 date, force definition, and country scope. The source-backed supplement remains the maintained register for accepted observations: military_personnel_supplemental_2001.tsv.

Future evidence can close a row if it gives a dated actual headcount (or a clearly scoped direct status count), identifies whether reserves, police, trainees, foreign personnel, or non-state forces are included, and covers the full country-level force represented by the game row.
